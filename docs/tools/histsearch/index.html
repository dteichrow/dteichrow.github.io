Maritime quarantine

How did ships, ports, and quarantine institutions shape exposure and public-health responses?

Mediterranean and Atlantic quarantine histories, with Grosse Île as a documented institutional case. This is a selective reading collection, not a systematic review.

Editorial note

Read the institutional history alongside the disease mechanism. A quarantine rule, a delay at anchor, and a death count describe different parts of the process. The Lind and Booker entries are metadata leads; they are not labeled as completed full-text reviews.

Revolutionary War smallpox

How did smallpox change military decisions, and what does the surviving correspondence establish?

Boston, the Canada campaign, and the Continental Army’s 1777 inoculation policy, with winter encampments as comparative context.

Editorial note

Verify the document itself after discovery: one secondary search result described the Adams–Cooper letter as June 9; the linked National Archives transcription is dated July 2, 1776. Orders document decisions; mortality effects require additional denominators and outcome records.

Curated dossier

Viking paleodemography

What can different skeletal and archaeological samples tell us about health, age at death, and movement?

Ribe, Birka, and Varnhem as contrasting study designs; selected North Atlantic and British sites as examples of chronology and sample selection.

Editorial note

Cemetery age-at-death distributions, an individual genome, dental prevalence, and dated wood are different kinds of evidence. Keep Viking Age, cross-period, and later Norse material separate. Abstract review is explicitly labeled where full tables were not reviewed.
