Who Counted Plague Deaths in Late-Medieval Cairo?

By April 1460, the inheritance bureau (Dīwān al-Mawārīth) in Cairo under the rule of the Mamluk Sultanate had reported 280 deaths. The funeral prayer ground near Bab al-Nasr, the gate on the northern wall of the city, had seen prayers said over 570 bodies on one Friday alone, the same day the inheritance bureau reported its tally. Mamluk historian Ibn Taghribirdi, son of a senior commander in the Sultanate, entered the two numbers in his records, noting that one prayer ground’s tally was already more than twice the bureau’s, even without the counts from any other prayer grounds.
