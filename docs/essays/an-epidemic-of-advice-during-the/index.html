An Epidemic of Advice During the English Sweat in 1529 Germany

In August of 1529, Martin Luther was worrying that the printed remedy for English sweating sickness going around was terrifying people who were just sweating for ordinary reasons. He claimed the pamphlet in question, meant to give people the information needed to identify the disease if it came around, could in reality turn that recognition into alarm bells. The sickness, and the information about it, was quickly making its way from one German city to the next.
